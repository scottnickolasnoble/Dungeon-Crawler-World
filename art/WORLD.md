# Downstairs: the world

This document holds everything around the main storyline (`STORY.md`):

- the gods and their temples
- the factions and their armies
- the other contestants
- the hidden clubs
- Floors 10 to 18

It's all original to Downstairs and grows out of things already in the game: the Builders, the Network, fans as fuel, the cone seals, and the monster camps.

---

## 1. The gods

### What a god is down here

The Stack is older than the Network. The things the Builders sealed into it (one under every floor) were never gods to begin with. They were **appetites**: hunger, fear of being forgotten, the urge to count, the urge to be watched. Attention feeds them. When the Network started broadcasting from the Stack, the appetites started getting fed, and they woke up. They gave themselves names and faces from the shows they'd overheard. Now they compete for the same thing the Network does: **your attention, and your fans'**.

The Network tolerates them because gods are great television.

**For writers:** the gods are real, they're powerful, and their gifts work. Every one of them is also a hunger that would eat the world if let out. A good ending needs some of them on your side anyway.

### Foreshadowing before Floor 7

From Floor 1 the player keeps finding small things that are these gods' early marks:

- Pylon's cone seals hold down a small piece of Saint Tally: the knocking that counts.
- The Lightwell moths keep Lady Lumen's old lamps.
- Grandma Gristle is a lapsed priestess of the Hollow Chef.
- The HOA prays to the Good Neighbour without knowing it.

**Shrines** (small, unattended, no worship) appear from Floor 2. Each one gives a line of lore and a one-off minor blessing.

### Temples and worship (Floor 7 onward)

- **Temples** are big set pieces: a priest NPC, an altar, an offering box and the god's statue. Temples on later floors keep a smaller "chapel" for every god met so far, so lapsed patrons can still be visited.
- **Choosing a patron:** after Floor 6 the player can swear to one god at its temple. They can switch at any later temple, but the old god's favour drops to zero and it sends a **Grudge**: a hunter party, a curse, or a ruined shop deal.
- **Favour (0–100)** rises through:
  - offerings (items the god likes)
  - acting in its domain (Tally: pay every debt, finish every count quest; Damp: rescue the drowned; Encore: get big fan spikes)
  - its **Rites**: a quest chain of 3–5 tasks per god
- **Boons at 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 favour:**
  - a passive blessing, e.g. Old Damp: you can breathe underwater and drowned enemies hesitate
  - an active **Miracle** on a long cooldown, e.g. Encore: "Standing Ovation", every fan watching heals you
  - a unique cosmetic for transmog
  - a god-only crafting recipe
- **Tithes:** each god takes a cut of something: a share of gold, fans, XP, or your sleep (Mother Mould). Patrons are never free.
- **Gods interfere:** rival gods' priests refuse to trade with you, their followers' camps get angrier, and at **Ratings Week** (below) your patron may intervene on your side, or ask a terrible favour.
- **Atheists** get no boons, but they get the **Unwatched** perk: gods can't see them, so god Grudges and curses can't find them. The hidden clubs like that.

### How factions use their armies

- **Raids:** every in-game day or so, a faction sends a raid party from its camp toward a rival's outposts or a town gate. Raid parties walk the map in formation (rabble in front, specialist in the middle, champion behind). You can watch, join one side, or rob both. This is the "stuff happening on the map at certain frequencies" from the design notes.
- **Territory:** outposts change hands. Kill a faction's champion and its rival takes the nearby outposts: the camp banners and units swap. Factions holding more territory field bigger raids.
- **Reputation (−100 to +100)** per faction:
  - **Hated:** they hunt you, and bounty parties appear.
  - **Neutral:** the default.
  - **Truced:** they won't attack first; Pylon's truce is the first of these.
  - **Allied:** you can trade at their camp, hire a unit as a follower, and they fight beside you in raids.
- **Ratings Week** (once per floor, after its main arc): the Network forces open war between that floor's factions for ratings. Every camp fields a full army, the map turns into a battlefield for a set time, and it ends in a big fight between the two leading factions. Your reputation decides which side counts you as theirs. Fans pour in, and so do the gods.

## 3. The other contestants

You're not the only one. Every stairwell on Earth emptied into the Stack, so every floor has **other contestants**: rivals, allies, victims and future bosses.

### How they work in the game

- **Named rivals (16)** have personalities, races and callings, and appear floor after floor. Their fan counts are on a **Leaderboard** that the Network shows between floors.
- **Generic contestants** (dozens per floor, built from the race models plus a random calling) wander, fight camps, loot and die. You'll find their bodies.
- **Parties:** up to three companions. Recruit a named rival who likes you, or hire a generic one at a town. Companions fight beside you (light AI using the same Mob system, on your side), level with you, comment on things, and can die for good. Each named one has a **loyalty** value: give them loot, back their choices and they stay. Betray them and they remember.
- **Crews:** named rivals form crews (contestant factions) with their own goals, and they compete with you for the same boss kills and boxes.

## 4. The hidden clubs (on every floor)

The Network sees almost everything. Almost. On every floor there's a place the cameras can't reach: a **club** behind a door that isn't on any map. It started as a maintenance cupboard the Builders forgot. Now it's a bar, a black market and a sanctuary, run by people who stopped going down.

### Three tiers, earned one at a time

**1. The Back Door** (from Floor 2)

- **What it is:** a dive bar and black market. A different hidden entrance on every floor: a freezer in Soup Row, a changing-room mirror in the Mall, a dog flap in Suburbia.
- **What's inside:**
  - **Mags**, the bartender, an ex-contestant who stopped on Floor 2 forty years ago
  - **the Fence:** sells contraband, buys anything, no Network tax
  - a **rumour board**: tips on hidden rooms, boss weaknesses and where rivals are
  - a **jukebox** (music and lore tapes)
  - **safe sleeping**: no camera, so no fan loss for resting
- **How you get in:** find the door (a hidden area, never marked), know the floor's **password** (from a lore note, a drowned contestant's bracelet, or a quest NPC who trusts you), and get **vouched for** once (Grandpa Mo or a Mags quest). After that the Back Door on every later floor knows you, but you still have to find it.

**2. The Lock-In** (from Floor 6)

- **What it is:** after hours in the Back Door, behind the cold-room door. A VIP lounge.
- **What's inside:**
  - a **gambling den**: cards, dice, and betting on fights between captured monsters
  - **contraband boxes**: off-Network loot boxes, unsponsored and sometimes cursed
  - a **bounty board**: kill named targets, including rival contestants
  - a **forger**, who changes your race once outside Floor 3 or scrubs your faction reputation (for a price)
  - **smugglers**, who sell routes: shortcuts between floors' hidden areas
- **How you get in:** earn **Marks**, the club's reputation. Marks come from:
  - errands for Mags (smuggling, delivering messages to other floors' Back Doors)
  - kills done **off-camera**: out of sight of every Network camera pole, a new mechanic that makes the camera poles matter
  - keeping your **fan count under a ceiling**, because the club doesn't trust Network darlings

  20 Marks earns the Lock-In key. Fans and Marks pull against each other, and you choose which you'd rather have.

**3. The Off-Air Room** (from Floor 10)

- **What it is:** the club's founders' room, behind the Lock-In's walk-in safe. No music, one table, a window onto a part of the Stack nobody has a camera in.
- **What's inside:**
  - the **Off-Air Society**: ex-contestants, a defected stagehand, Brother Kettle, and on Floor 16 a Network engineer
  - the true story of the Network
  - the **Builders' maps**
  - the **sabotage** questline that leads to one of the endings
- **How you get in:** 60 Marks, plus a Mags quest on Floor 9 (get her old crew out of the Undermayor's vault), plus **no god as patron**, or a patron who has turned on the Network (Old Damp after his Rites, or the Index).

## 5. Floors 10 to 18

There are three acts from here. The floors get bigger (1,650 m on Floor 10, up to 2,050 m on Floor 18), stranger, and more tied to the gods, the factions and the truth.

### Act Four: the Spectacle (Floors 10–12)
*Where the show is made. You're famous now, and it's worse.*

**Floor 10: The Backlot**
- **Feel:** a studio backlot that goes on forever. Fake towns that are only fronts (a western street, a moon base, a Victorian alley), giant sound stages, catering tents, bleachers full of an audience that never moves.
- **Hub:** Craft Services, a catering-tent town.
- **Villages:** Wardrobe Row, the Props Warehouse, Extras' Holding, the Car Park.
- **Camps:**
  - Sound Stage 4 (the Floor Manager and Stagehands)
  - the Western Set (the Stunt Double and Extras)
  - Makeup Trailers (the Makeup Witch and Boom Gremlins)
  - Catering (the Craft Services Glutton)
  - the Water Tank (the Showrunner and the Crew; floor boss)
- **Landmarks:** the Giant Clapperboard, the Fake Moon, the Audience Bleachers.
- **Temple:** the Antlered Viewer's Stadium Chapel, inside a camera crane.
- **Back Door:** behind a fake saloon front that is, for once, real.
- **Story:** your first season "episode" is filmed on you; Dex is filming his.

**Floor 11: The Arena**
- **Feel:** a stadium city: a colosseum the size of a district, concourses, fan zones, locker rooms, burger stands, mascots.
- **Hub:** the Concourse.
- **Villages:** the Away End, the Car Wash, the Sports Bar, the Ticket Office.
- **Camps:**
  - the Mascot Pen (the Big Foam Finger and Mascots)

- **Story:**
  - Ratings Week is a literal tournament.

**Floor 12: The Green Room**
- **Feel:** backstage forever. Corridors of dressing rooms with stars on the doors, a talk-show set, the Writers' Room, a canned-laughter factory, makeup mirrors with bulbs.

*Where the Builders kept their records. The show stops being the scariest thing.*

## 7. Discovery: camps, contestants, secrets and puzzles (every floor)

On floors this big, finding things *is* the game. Every floor gets these, scaled to its size (roughly one of something every 120 m in any direction).

| Kind | Per floor (Floor 4 → Floor 18) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Boss camps | 4–5 | A faction's stronghold, pushed far out |
| Outposts | 12 → 30 | Chains of small camps on the way, in varied patterns |
| **Contestant camps** | 3 → 8 | A crew's tents and fire. Some trade, heal or recruit; the Scavengers ambush you; some camps are already dead when you get there |
| **Hidden encampments** | 2 → 6 | Not on any map, behind a puzzle. Lore, loot, and a piece of the story |
| Points of interest | 10 → 25 | Shrines, wrecks, lookouts, hideouts, nests, sights that belong to the nearby camps |
| Summits and towers | 6 → 14 | Height, and a stash for whoever climbs |
| The Back Door | 1 | The club's hidden entrance (see section 4) |

### Hidden encampments

Each one is a small camp or chamber (a dead crew's last stand, a Builders' maintenance room, a god's forgotten shrine, a previous season's set), sealed by a **puzzle**. Its **clues** are elsewhere on the floor: notes on field-notes boards, a drowned contestant's bracelet, graffiti, a song on the club jukebox.

| Puzzle | How it plays | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Symbol lock** | Press symbols in the right order (like the Floor 1 ticket machine) | Three scraps, each with a symbol and a number or time |
| **Lamps in order** | Light four braziers in the right order; wrong order and they all go out | A rhyme or a stained-glass picture |
| **Lever pattern** | Set five levers up or down | A punched card, a tally of marks |
| **Password door** | Say the word at a speaking grille | A word underlined in someone's journal |
| **Statues facing** | Turn three statues to face the right landmarks | "They all looked at the …" |
| **Hidden switch** | Something that isn't quite right (a crooked painting, a loose brick) opens a way down | Graffiti points at it |

**Rewards:**
- a **lore page** (see below)
- a stash
- sometimes **god favour** (a forgotten shrine)
- sometimes club **Marks**, because the club pays for secrets

### Lore pages: the story told in pieces

There's a set of pages per act. Collect them and the hidden story assembles in your journal:
- **Act One (Floors 1–3), "The Builders' Mark":** the symbol, the seals, the building that was here first.
- **Act Two (Floors 4–6), "Past Seasons":** contestants before you, bracelets, the dates that don't make sense.
- **Act Three (Floors 7–9), "What They Sealed":** each god as an appetite, and why the Builders locked them in.
- **Act Four (Floors 10–12), "How the Show is Made":** fans are measured, then harvested.
- **Act Five (Floors 13–15), "The Archive":** the Builders' own records, and the first contestant.
- **Act Six (Floors 16–18), "The Host":** who he was, and what winning means.

Finding enough pages unlocks extra dialogue with Marta, Mags and the Off-Air Society, and is required for the **truth** endings of bosses (like Pylon's).
