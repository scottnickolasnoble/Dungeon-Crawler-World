# Downstairs: the storyline

This is the story bible. It keeps every floor theme, character and quest already in the game, and turns them into one continuous mystery instead of "town, camps, boss, stairs".

## How every floor is built

Each floor has five layers. The boss is the end of the floor's story, not just its strongest monster.

1. **Survival problem.** Food, water, shelter, light, sickness, the environment.
2. **Civilisation problem.** The town, its factions, merchants, guilds and other contestants, and what they want from you.
3. **Mystery.** Something on the floor doesn't make sense.
4. **Dungeon mechanism.** A puzzle, old machine, ritual, collection or route. Each floor uses a different kind, so they never feel like the same puzzle twice.
5. **Boss.** Whoever controls or protects the floor's central problem. Most bosses have three endings: **kill**, **bargain**, or **uncover the truth**, which needs the floor's knowledge and relationships.

## Four kinds of progress

| Kind | How you earn it | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| Combat | Kill monsters and bosses | XP, gear, power |
| Fans | Put on a show: big kills, near deaths, drama, style | Fan boxes, sponsors, Network favour |
| Knowledge | Investigate, read, solve, notice | Secrets, other ways past problems, the real story |
| Relationships | Help people and factions, keep promises | Allies, truces, changes on later floors |

**The central tension:** the audience loves violence and spectacle, and the truth is found through mercy and curiosity. Killing the boss earns the most fans. Uncovering the truth earns the least fans and the most knowledge. A player who kills everything can finish the game. A player who looks closer gets a different, stranger story and a different ending. The Network notices which kind of player you are.

**What players should feel, act by act:**

| Floors | The player thinks | What is really going on |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Undercroft | "We're trapped. I need food, water and a way down." | Contestants are being sorted and watched. |
| 2. Laundromat | "This place has rules and machinery." | The dungeon is ancient and automated. The Network found it; it didn't build it. |
| 3. Soup District | "We're contestants in a game, and the game changes people." | The player is being selected, shaped and manipulated. |
| 4–6 | "The Network is selling us." | Sponsors, audiences and hunters feed on the contestants. |
| 7–9 | "There's a civilisation down here that knows the truth." | The Undercity is made of the survivors of past seasons. |
| 10–18 | "The show is the machine." | The audience's attention is what powers the dungeon. |

## The truth (spoilers, for writers only)

Every clue in the game points back at this. Players piece it together slowly; nobody ever explains it outright.

- **The dungeon is older than the Network.** It's a vast machine, here called **the Stack**, built by a vanished people (the **Builders**). Its job was to remake a world's population: sort people, give each one a function (worker, merchant, guardian, monster) and build a society out of them, floor by floor. The Builders' mark is the symbol players keep finding.
- **The Network didn't build the Stack. It found it.** It can't fully control the machine, so it runs it as a game show. The machine needs a fresh population every cycle, and **the audience's attention is the power it runs on**. That's what fans really are, and it's why the Network needs spectacle and doesn't care who dies.
- **Earth isn't the first world.** Every season is another planet "acquired". Some survivors of earlier seasons are still down here, absorbed into the dungeon as townsfolk, custodians, merchants and bosses. Old Reggie clocked in in 1986, Earth time, because the Stack keeps its own clock across seasons.
- **The Network sealed the old routes.** Traffic cones are **Network-grade seal plastic**, used to contain machinery the Network doesn't understand. The sideways routes between floors, the Great Dryer and the Hall of Callings' old machines are all things the Network has fenced off.
- **Floor 18 isn't on the Network's map.** The bottom of the Stack is its original control room. The Network has never reached it. "Winning" the show as the Network defines it means reaching the Network's last floor and becoming part of the show.
- **The Host is a past winner.** He won an earlier season and was made the face of the show. The glitches show his real face. He's afraid of the old machinery, and of contestants who ask questions, because he knows where that leads, and because part of him wants them to succeed.

**The ending choice (Floor 18):** take the Network's prize and become the next Host; shut the Stack down, which frees the people inside it but may cost Earth's survivors their way home; or take control of the Stack and turn the show on the Network. Which endings you can reach depends on your knowledge and relationships, not just your level.

## Running threads through Floors 1–3

- **The symbol.** The Builders' mark appears on the Floor 1 ticket machine, Floor 2's employee badge and Floor 3's Scratch Marks. The player learns it means "the building was here first".
- **The scratch marks.** Human tally marks near every way down, from contestants of every season. On Floor 3 they turn out to be pieces of one map of the whole Stack.
- **Cones as seals.** A running joke that turns out to be load-bearing: from Pickles' "key" to Pylon's toll to the Cone Cathedral.
- **Dave.** He has been eaten before. He'll be eaten again. "Don't trust the dryers."
- **Network notices.** A cheerful interruption after every discovery. They get slightly less cheerful each floor.

## Floor 0: The Landing (orientation)

**Feel:** Bureaucratic, bright, wrong. A TV lobby that's a little too eager.

- **Quests (kept):** *Orientation*, *Contestant Basics*, *Cardboard Combat*, *Going Down* (all from Intern Pip).
- **The tutorial teaches everything the game uses:**
  - movement, attacks, dodging and stamina (cardboard goblins)
  - the skill tree
  - crafting and material tracking (Pip hands out a starter kit and a "Network Materials Ledger")
  - safe rooms, food and water
  - fans: your first kill earns a few viewers, and Pip celebrates far too much
- **First seed of the mystery:** Pip's orientation video skips a section. If you look at the screen when it skips, you see a frame of the symbol. Pip says the video "does that sometimes" and quickly changes the subject.
- **Exit:** the stairs down. Pip says goodbye like someone who has said it many times, and doesn't expect to see anyone again.

## Floor 1: The Undercroft (survive)

**Feel:** Darkness, confusion, community. A car park the size of a town, lit in pockets.

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | Water (the camp's water line keeps failing), food, light, safe ground. "Don't wander into the dark." |
| Civilisation | Stairwell Camp: Marta, Pickles, Bo, Rusty Kell, Dot and frightened new arrivals. |
| Mystery | The Exit Sign leads to a wall, and human scratch marks surround the doorway, one with a date from before this season. |
| Mechanism | The parking attendant's ticket machine. |
| Boss | Sergeant Pylon, who guards the chained stairwell, though not for the reason anyone thinks. |

### Opening

You arrive at Stairwell Camp in your bathrobe. Marta Vance doesn't send you to kill anything. She tells you the first rule: **nobody knows how to leave.** The first survivors spent days searching. Someone found a glowing EXIT sign and everyone celebrated, but behind the door was solid concrete. "It isn't an exit. It's bait."

### Main arc: "Welcome to the Dungeon"

1. **Welcome to the Dungeon** (Marta). Where to sleep, where the water is, how to find food, how the map works, safe ground versus monster territory. "Don't wander into the dark." You learn why the first time you do.
2. **Nobody Gets Out** (Marta). Find the Exit Sign, open the door and find the wall. Around the doorway are hundreds of human scratch marks, old and fresh. One has a date carved beside it, from before this season of Downstairs.
3. **The ticket machine** (new, optional; the first puzzle). An abandoned attendant's booth near the Exit Sign holds a machine with 18 level buttons, though the Undercroft only seems to have a few levels. Three damaged parking tickets nearby each carry a symbol, a number and a time. They're instructions. Enter the symbols in the right order and the screen reads: *THE BUILDING WAS HERE BEFORE THE CONTESTANTS.* A Network notice follows at once:

   > NETWORK NOTICE: Congratulations, contestant! You have discovered a thing. Things are not currently redeemable for fan boxes.

4. **Find the Leak** (Marta). The camp's water is going missing. The trail leads to Pylon Point and the goblins (see *Thin the Packs*).
5. **The Chained Stairwell** (Marta). The way down is chained, and Pylon has the key. This is now the floor's main question: get past Pylon.

### Side arc: the goblins (*Thin the Packs*)

The goblins are attacking the water line. You follow them and find out why: they're stealing valves to build their own crude irrigation, because they need water too. You choose:

- **Kill them.** Water is safe and the camp is grateful. Big fans, and Pylon's camp gets angrier.
- **Repair the valve and share the water.** The camp grumbles, and the goblins stop raiding. A truce; goblin outposts become passable.
- **Let Pickles negotiate.** This opens Pickles' arc early and makes the bargain ending with Pylon easier.

The lesson for the whole game: **not everything you fight has to die.**

### Pickles' arc: *Cone Collector* and *Cone Coup*

- **Cone Collector.** Pickles wants 12 pieces of cone plastic. It looks like a joke, until he reveals he's making a key. The cones are Network-grade plastic, and the dungeon's machinery behaves strangely around them.
- **Cone Coup.** Pickles wants Pylon removed. The twist: Pylon's cone stacks aren't decoration. They're a crude seal over something under Pylon Point, and Pylon charges a toll because he's terrified of what happens if anyone breaks them.

### Bo's arc: *Bo's Toolbox*

Bo's toolbox is at the Ramp Spiral, but the goblins have taken the springs. Tracking them leads into a maintenance tunnel, where a vending machine is moving: not attacking, just moving. Follow it and it walks through a wall that simply opens. This is the floor's first "what the hell is this place?" moment, and it foreshadows the Vending Mimics and the Machine That Ate Dave.

### The camps have purpose

- **The Pigeon Loft.** The Pigeon Elder remembers "the ones who went down" before this season, but not where they went. Killing him earns fans; listening to him earns a clue to Floor 3.
- **The Lightwell.** The moths are maintaining the floor's lights. Kill the Moth Matron and the Lightwell goes dark, so the region gets more dangerous. Bring her a lasting light instead, and she opens hidden maintenance tunnels: a shortcut and a loot route.
- **The Flooded Bay.** The Big Sucker is guarding a body, not treasure. It belongs to a contestant who died years ago, and their bracelet is still receiving Network notifications. If you read it, the last one says: *"Contestant, you have been selected."*
- **The Lost Property Office.** Lost & Found has kept every item ever lost on this floor, from every season, some labelled in languages that aren't from Earth.

### Boss: Sergeant Pylon

Pylon is a joke boss who's genuinely dangerous: loud, stupid, obsessed with cones, and just trying to survive. He isn't evil.

- **Kill him.** It's fast and earns big fans. His cone seals collapse and something under Pylon Point wakes. It becomes a harder fight on the way back up, and it changes the Undercroft's monster camps.
- **Bargain** (needs Pickles' arc). Pylon hands over the key and admits he's been terrified of what's under the stairwell. The seal holds, and he becomes a grudging ally who turns up again later.
- **The truth** (needs the ticket machine and the Flooded Bay clue). Pylon shows you the seal himself. You learn the cones are holding something in, and Pickles finishes his key: a cone-plastic key that opens Network seals on later floors.

### The reveal and the ending

When Pylon's stairwell opens, the dungeon announces: **FLOOR COMPLETION CONDITION: 1/1.** Nobody knew there was a condition. The Host appears, applauding. "Oh! You found the secret." A pause. "There isn't one." The wall opens behind him. This is the first time the player realises the dungeon has been tracking them all along.

Marta watches you go. "Come back if you can." Then: "Most don't."

## Floor 2: The Laundromat (understand)

**Feel:** Routine. Clean, bright and still working, which is much creepier than darkness.

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | Bleach, steam, scalding water, slippery floors. Clean water is everywhere but most of it is soapy, so drinking water must be found or filtered. |
| Civilisation | The laundromat town; Old Reggie; Spin Cycle; Dave (when not eaten). |
| Mystery | The laundromat is still processing contestants, and people who died long ago still have washes booked. |
| Mechanism | The Great Dryer: a sequence put together from symbols on laundry found across the floor. |
| Boss | Head Custodian Ebb, who believes the whole dungeon is his workplace and everyone in it is a spill. |

### Opening

Everything is running: machines turning, lights on, music playing, no monsters. A voice says "WELCOME TO CUSTOMER SERVICE." The doors lock and a screen lights up: **CURRENT LOAD: 4,117,021 CONTESTANTS**, the number who reached the stairs on Earth this season. The laundromat has been processing them, and nobody knows what that means.

### Main arc: Old Reggie (*Clocking Out*, *End of Shift*)

- **Clocking Out.** Reggie says he mopped these floors for forty years. You ask when he came down. He pauses: "I don't know." In Boiler Room 0 you find hundreds of timecards with thousands of names: some from Earth, some from species you've never seen, and some from contestants who haven't arrived yet. Reggie's card reads **CLOCKED IN: 1986.**
- **End of Shift.** Reggie wants to finish his shift and leave. Choose:
  - **Let him clock out.** He leaves and doesn't come back. A Network notice reads: *"Employee reassigned."*
  - **Tear up his card.** He's free and terrified, and he becomes an ally on later floors who knows the old routes.
  - **Leave it.** He keeps mopping. He's the only character on the floor who's always there for you.

**The first real dungeon rule:** the dungeon doesn't simply kill people. It assigns them functions, such as worker, monster, merchant, boss or custodian.

### Side arcs

- **Coin Jar Raid** (Spin Cycle) and **the Machine That Ate Dave.** The coin jars lead you to the vending machine that ate Dave. Nobody's worried, because Dave has been eaten before. Defeat it and Dave climbs out, alive, more or less. "Oh. You're new." Then: "Don't trust the dryers."
- **The Lint King.** The lint traps are an ecosystem: lint is insulation, crafting material, monster nests, fire starters and a magical ingredient. The Lint King believes it's all his. You can kill him, bargain, steal it, or help clean the machines, which earns a steady lint supply and a truce.
- **The drying yards.** Shapes behind the sheets. Most are just laundry, and some aren't.

### Mechanism: the Great Dryer

The Great Dryer is enormous, and nobody knows what it does. It has 18 settings, 18 symbols, 18 temperature controls and a timer counting backward from an unknown number. Laundry across the floor carries symbols; collecting it lets you rebuild the sequence. The dryer isn't for clothes. It's a transport system between floors. Put something in, and the screen reads **DESTINATION: FLOOR 18**, then **ERROR: FLOOR 18 DOES NOT EXIST.** The Host cuts in, "Oops! Let's not play with the old machinery, contestants," and shuts it down. It's the first time he seems uncomfortable. (Later in the game, the dryer becomes a shortcut that sends items between floors.)

### Boss: Head Custodian Ebb

Ebb is a broken employee, still doing his job. Contestants are spills, monsters are messes. "You are tracking mud on my floor." Then: "Shift change, wights!" and dead custodians answer.

- **Kill him.** The doors open, and the Mop Wights keep cleaning without him, more aggressively.
- **Bargain.** Clock him out. He leaves his post, and the floor stops processing new arrivals for a while.
- **The truth** (needs Reggie's arc). Show him Reggie's card. Ebb realises he was a contestant once too, and gives you his keyring, which opens old service doors on later floors.

### Ending

Before descending, you find an old employee badge carrying the symbol from the Floor 1 ticket machine. The floors are connected. There's one system underneath everything.

## Floor 3: The Soup District (choose)

**Feel:** The first real civilisation: markets, music, children, street performers. Finally, a place to live, until the Dinner Bell.

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | The Dinner Bell. Every night everyone must eat. Refuse and you sicken; eat the wrong soup and you change. Soup has real effects, some good. |
| Civilisation | Soup Row; Wickley; Brother Ash; the Guild Hall (Archmage Pell, Foreman Gruss, Supervisor Mox, Matron Hale); Judge Vrask; Registrar Odessa. |
| Mystery | Grandma Gristle's and Auntie Brine's soups come from one ancient recipe, and the Scratch Marks are a map. |
| Mechanism | The First Broth: rebuilding a recipe from tablets found across the district. Also the Hall of Callings' old machines. |
| Boss | The Cone King, keeper of the Cone Cathedral and the machinery under it. |

### Arrival

Soup Row is full of contestants having fun. Someone mentions, "Dinner Bell's in twenty minutes." You ask what that is, and everyone goes quiet. **The Dinner Bell** is the floor's recurring system: soup buffs and changes you, and you learn which soups to trust.

### The Hall of Callings (race and calling)

The biggest moment of Act One. Registrar Odessa explains that every contestant who reaches Floor 3 chooses a race and a calling. "Why Floor 3?" "Because that's when the dungeon decides you're worth investing in."

- **Choosing a race** is a story event: the old machines wake and show possible versions of you. The Host cuts in, "Choose carefully!", and Odessa goes pale: "He shouldn't be able to see this room." Avatar customisation happens here as well as at the start.
- **Choosing a calling** is a trial, done through *Guild Trial I–III* (kept). Each class is tested by doing what it does:
  - Pyromancer: keep a flame alive while monsters try to put it out.
  - Warrior: protect someone for a few minutes.
  - Rogue: steal something unseen.
  - Mage: solve a magical puzzle.
  - Healer: keep several people alive.

### Main arcs

- **Soup Feud** (Wickley). Grandma Gristle's soup has bones, memories and old magic; Auntie Brine's has salt, magic and something newer. Taste both. Then the twist: both come from one ancient recipe that has gone wrong. Recipe tablets around the district carry an ingredient, a temperature, a cooking time and a symbol. Rebuild **the First Broth**, and you learn it was never food. It was used to make life.
- **Lights Out** and **The Last Light** (Brother Ash). The moths aren't attacking. They're starving for light, because the Lamp Market is going dark. Kill the Bulb Queen, feed her, or find out why she's starving: the dungeon's light system is failing floor by floor. That's the best route, and a thread for later floors.
- **Five Stars** (Judge Vrask). Vrask judges the district's food and keeps the peace with taste. Earning five stars opens his support in the Cone King's ending.
- **Old Man Coo.** He saw contestants go sideways, not down. The Stack used to have routes between floors, and the Network sealed them.
- **The Scratch Marks.** The marks from Floors 1, 2 and 3 fit together into a map of the whole Stack. The dungeon was built as one machine.

### Grandma Gristle

The first boss the player should question killing. She knows about the First Broth and what the scratches mean, and she refuses to say, because she's protecting something.

- **Kill her.** You get her loot and the fans, but the secret dies with her.
- **Investigate her kitchen.** You find a cookbook older than this season, with a recipe titled **CONTESTANT**. This is the floor's big horror reveal.

### Boss: the Cone King (*Long Live the Cone*)

He knows Pylon, the Undercroft and the old system. He smells your equipment: "You have been down below. The old things smell different." Pylon wasn't the first Sergeant; there have been hundreds, one every season, and the dungeon replaces them. The Cone Cathedral is built of traffic cones, and under it is ancient machinery. He isn't worshipping cones. He's guarding the machine, and the cones are seals.

- **Kill him.** The seals break and the stairs open, and something changes back on Floor 1.
- **Bargain.** He gives you the key if you promise to keep the seals intact.
- **The truth** (needs enough Floor 1–3 knowledge and allies). Convince him the Network is manipulating everyone, and he gives you an **Ancient Token**, which lets you use old dungeon machinery on later floors.

### The Act One ending (first big cutscene)

You walk towards the stairs. The Host appears to applause and confetti: "THREE FLOORS!" Then he stops, looks straight at you, and his smile shrinks a little. "You're asking questions." The screen glitches, and for half a second there's a different face. Then he's back. "Don't." Cut to black.

**What the player now knows:** the dungeon is ancient, the Network didn't build all of it, past contestants have been here for decades, people get absorbed into the dungeon, the floors are connected, the Host doesn't control everything and is afraid of something, the player is being watched and judged, and there are ways through besides killing.

**What they still don't know:** who built it, what Floor 18 is, why Earth was taken, why the Network needs contestants, where the earlier contestants went, and whether anyone wants them to reach the bottom.

## Act Two: Floors 4–6, the show (sold)

The player starts working the system. The question changes from "what is this?" to "who profits?"

### Floor 4: The Endless Mall (sponsorship)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | Food courts serve endless free food, which is addictive and slowly changes you. Real food has to be bought. |
| Civilisation | The Food Court Free State; Clearance Carl, Cinnamon Sue, Lost Receipts; Mattress Mo's inn; Sunny the Sponsor Rep. |
| Mystery | The mall's "veins" all lead to the Atrium, and the Mall Mother is alive. |
| Mechanism | Sponsorship. Sponsors give you power with conditions attached ("wear our logo", "kill three on camera"). |
| Boss | The Mall Mother, the heart that keeps the mall alive. |

- **Quests:** *Sponsored Content* (Sunny, main) and *Lost Child Announcement* (Madame Prickle).
- **The story:** Sunny signs you to a sponsor. Sponsorship is the fan system made physical: power now, with conditions you discover later. Timmy, the lost child, is real and hiding in the Changing Rooms. He's one of the few children who survived this long, and the mall is trying to keep him. The dummies are past shoppers.
- **The boss:** the Mall Mother is a colossal heart fused with the mall, calm and motherly. She keeps everyone fed, safe and still. **Kill** her and the mall dies around you. **Bargain** to let people leave. **The truth:** she was the mall's first contestant, and she's keeping them from going deeper because she knows what's below.

### Floor 5: Suburbia Infinite (conformity)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | The HOA fines you for anything and everything. Unpaid fines become monsters. |
| Civilisation | Fort Cul-de-Sac; Big Deb; Yard Sale Yolanda, Lemonade Lee, Curbside Cal, Mrs Doily; the Neighbourhood Watch. |
| Mystery | The same family waves from every yard, and the houses further out have gone wrong. |
| Mechanism | The HOA rulebook. Rules can be learned, exploited and rewritten through the town council. |
| Boss | The HOA President; Mayor Eternal at Town Hall. |

- **Quests:** *Notice of Violation* (Big Deb, main) and *Good Boy* (the Watch: Mr. Fetch was somebody's dog once).
- **The story:** Suburbia is the Stack's idea of a perfect society, with everyone in their function. The Hendersons are what happens to contestants who settle down. Mr. Fetch's collar has a name tag from Earth.
- **The boss:** the HOA President. **Kill** her: the fines vanish and the gnomes revolt. **Bargain:** a seat on the council. **The truth:** Mayor Eternal has been re-elected every season since the Stack began. He's a Builder relic and the first character who knows who the Builders were. He won't say, but he recognises the Ancient Token.

### Floor 6: The Canopy (the hunted)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | The dark forest; you are prey; you must hide your scent. |
| Civilisation | The Treehouse; Gusset; Elder Lantern and the Mothwing; Bark & Blade, Forager Fern, Twig; Old Bramble's inn. |
| Mystery | The roots at the Tallest Tree go somewhere that isn't down. |
| Mechanism | The hunt. The Glimmerkin run paid safaris for audience members, and you learn to turn the hunt around. |
| Boss | Lord-Hunter Facet at the Safari Lodge. |

- **Quests:** *Hunting Season* (Gusset, main), *Lodge Fire* (Gusset, main) and *The Roots of Things* (Elder Lantern, arc).
- **The story:** the hunters are audience members, paying to come down and kill contestants. The audience is real, physical and here. *The Roots of Things* reveals that the Tallest Tree's roots are a sealed sideways route: the first one the player can actually open, with Pickles' cone key or the Ancient Token.
- **The boss:** Lord-Hunter Facet. **Kill** him: the hunts stop, and the Network sends harder hunters later. **Bargain:** become a hunter guide; you earn fans and lose allies. **The truth:** the lodge's records show the audience is paying in attention, and attention is what's spent below. This is the first hint that fans are fuel.

## Act Three: Floors 7–9, the Undercity (the survivors)

A whole civilisation built from survivors of past seasons. The Undercity knows much of the truth, and has made its peace with it.

### Floor 7: The Drowned Cistern (debt)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | The flood. Water level rises and falls on a timer: a timed map event. |
| Civilisation | Gangway; Nan Gangway's ferry; tenants and the Landlord. |
| Mystery | Everyone here owes rent, and nobody remembers borrowing anything. |
| Mechanism | The sluices and the chain ferry, used to control the water level and open drowned districts. |
| Boss | The Sanitation King on the Landfill Barge; the Landlord and Old Lady Bathwater on the way. |

- **The story:** debt is how the Undercity keeps survivors in their functions. Nan Gangway ferries people across for free and has done so for three seasons.

### Floor 8: The Workings (labour)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | Heat, air and exhaustion. Rest is rationed. |
| Civilisation | Shaft Town; Foreman Tuck; the mole-kin; the Collectors. |
| Mystery | Eleven miners went down and twelve came up. |
| Mechanism | The drill and the mine carts, used to dig your own route through. |
| Boss | Big Momma Mold in the Nursery; on the way, Chief Grubtooth, the Drill Prophet, the Fryer Baron and Repossessor Mk. IX. |

- **The story:** the Workings is where the Stack's machinery is maintained. The miners are digging towards the Builders' level, and Big Momma Mold tells everyone they've done enough. She's keeping them from finding it.

### Floor 9: The Undercity (power)

| Layer | On this floor |
|---|---|
| Survival | The election. Everyone must vote, and the losing side is "redistributed". |
| Civilisation | Verdigris Square; the Rat Senate; Sister Verdigris; the city's old families from past seasons. |
| Mystery | The Undermayor has never been elected, and the Founders Lantern burns something alive. |
| Mechanism | The vote, which can be rigged, campaigned or overturned. |
| Boss | The Undermayor at City Hall; the Rat Throne King, Lockjaw and the Soloist on the way. |

- **The story:** the Undercity's founders were past contestants who chose to stop going down. The Undermayor runs the city for the Network. **The truth** ending opens the sealed Metro, the sideways route towards the deep floors, and turns the Undercity into an ally for Acts Four and Five.

## Acts Four and Five: Floors 10–18 (sketch)

- **Floors 10–12, the Spectacle.** The arenas and studios where the show is made. The player becomes famous. The mystery is how the broadcast actually works.
- **Floors 13–15, the Deep Archive.** The Builders' records. The player learns what the Stack was for, and that fans are fuel.
- **Floors 16–18, the Network Core.** The Network's control floors, and the Host's past. Then the floor that "does not exist": the Builders' control room, and the ending choice.
